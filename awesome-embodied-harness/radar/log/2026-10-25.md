## Signals

- **The human stays in the loop during deployment, not just before it.**
  - TIDE updates a robot foundation policy online from human interventions and its own rollouts, with no critic or reward model, and makes new checkpoints available within an hour (first-party).
  - TEAR shifts control between teleoperation and local autonomy from network and task evidence, keeping teleoperation to about a fifth to a quarter of control steps (first-party).
  - SimPT automates the same intervention loop in simulation, so post-training algorithms can be compared without hours of human supervision.
- **World models should be trained for the policy they serve.** DRILL (NeurIPS 2026) updates a video world model to maximize the VLA's return after an imagined-rollout update. It argues lower pixel error can produce worse policy gradients, and reports gains over WMPO and RLVR-World (first-party).
